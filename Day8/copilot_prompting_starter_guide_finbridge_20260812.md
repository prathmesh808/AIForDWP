# Copilot Prompting Starter Guide
**Date:** 2026-08-12  
**Audience:** FinBridge office staff  
**Prepared by:** DWP IT Support

This guide is for everyday Microsoft 365 users who want better results from Copilot without needing technical knowledge. The quickest way to improve Copilot's answers is to give it a little more context and be clear about the result you want.

---

## A simple prompt formula to remember

Use this easy pattern:

**Ask + What it's using + What you need + How it should look**

In plain English, that means:

- **Ask:** What do you want Copilot to do?
- **What it's using:** Is it looking at an email, a document, or a spreadsheet?
- **What you need:** Do you want a summary, a draft reply, key actions, or something else?
- **How it should look:** Do you want bullet points, a short email, or a table?

### Quick example

Instead of writing:

> Summarise this

Try:

> Summarise this email thread, highlight the decisions already made, and list any actions still assigned to me in 5 bullet points.

That one extra sentence usually gives a much more useful result.

## Outlook prompt examples

Use Outlook prompts when you need to catch up quickly, reply faster, or turn a long thread into clear next steps.

### Example 1: Catch up on a long thread

> Summarise this email thread for me. Focus on the latest updates, any decisions already made, and anything I still need to respond to. Present the answer as 3 short sections: Updates, Decisions, and My Actions.

### Example 2: Draft a reply

> Draft a polite reply to this email confirming that I have reviewed the request, that the finance report will be sent by 3 pm today, and that I will contact the sender if there are any delays. Keep the tone professional and concise.

### Example 3: Prepare for the day

> Review my recent emails from the last 2 working days and identify anything urgent, anything waiting for my reply, and any deadlines mentioned. Show the result as a simple priority list.

### Why these work

These prompts tell Copilot what to look at, what matters most, and how to present the result so you can act on it quickly.

## Word prompt examples

Use Word prompts when you need help drafting, rewriting, or tightening up business documents.

### Example 1: Turn notes into a draft

> Turn these meeting notes into a clear first draft of a follow-up note for the team. Include a short summary of what was discussed, the agreed actions, and the target dates. Keep the tone professional and easy to read.

### Example 2: Rewrite for a different audience

> Rewrite this update for a non-technical audience at FinBridge. Use plain language, shorten long sentences, and keep the message friendly but professional.

### Example 3: Improve structure

> Review this document and improve the structure. Add clear headings, tighten repeated points, and suggest a shorter opening paragraph that gets to the point faster.

### Why these work

These prompts do more than ask for a rewrite. They explain the audience, the purpose, and the style you want.

---

## Excel prompt examples

Use Excel prompts when you want help spotting trends, explaining data, or preparing something you can share with others.

### Example 1: Explain the main story in the data

> Review this sales table and tell me the main trends by month, any obvious drops or spikes, and which figures may need a closer look. Summarise the answer in plain language for a manager.

### Example 2: Help prepare a summary

> Create a short summary of this spreadsheet showing total spend by department, the 3 highest-cost areas, and any unusual values that stand out. Present the result as bullet points I can use in an email.

### Example 3: Make the sheet easier to use

> Look at this project tracker and help me show 3 things clearly: overdue items, tasks due this week, and which team owns each task. Suggest a simple way to lay this out so it is easier to review in a meeting.

### Why these work

These prompts help Copilot focus on business meaning, not just raw numbers. They are especially useful when you need a quick summary before speaking to a colleague or manager.

---

## A few easy habits that improve results

- Start with the task, not the tool. Say what you need done first.
- Mention the audience if that matters, for example a manager, customer, or wider team.
- Ask for a format you can use straight away, such as bullet points, a short email, or a table.
- If the first answer is too broad, follow up with one more instruction such as: make this shorter, make it more formal, or focus only on actions.

### Follow-up prompt examples

> Make that version shorter and more direct.

> Rewrite that for a senior manager with less detail.

> Keep the same summary but turn the actions into a numbered list.

## Data safety reminder

Please keep the **Day 1 data-safety habit** in mind when using AI tools.

Using **Microsoft 365 Copilot at FinBridge** is part of normal business use, so you do not need to be worried about using it for everyday work you are authorised to do.

The main caution is about **external AI tools**. Do not paste confidential client data, account details, personal data, or sensitive business information into public or unapproved AI chat tools.

If you are unsure whether a tool is approved, pause and check with IT first.

Good habit to remember:

> Copilot in our Microsoft 365 environment is for normal work. External AI tools need extra care.

---

## A final tip

You do not need a perfect prompt on the first try. Start with a clear request, then refine it once Copilot gives you a draft. In practice, the best prompts are usually simple, specific, and tied to a real piece of work you are doing.
